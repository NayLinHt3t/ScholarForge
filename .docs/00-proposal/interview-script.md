# Interview Script — ScholarForge

A ready-to-run moderator script, paired with [interview-plan.md](interview-plan.md) (rationale, hypotheses, recruitment, analysis criteria). Use this document *during* the session; use the plan document to design/analyze the round.

## Before the session
- Have `.docs/02-design/prototype.html` open in a browser tab, ready to share your screen.
- Ask the participant beforehand (by message, not live) for **one real research idea they're actually working on** — you'll type their own idea into the Search screen live. A borrowed real idea makes the relevance reaction meaningful; a made-up demo idea doesn't.
- Opening script (read or paraphrase):
  > "Thanks for doing this. I'm going to ask about how you currently do research, then show you a rough prototype and get your honest reactions — including if you think this is a bad idea, that's genuinely useful to hear. Okay if I record audio just for my notes? This won't be shared outside the project."

---

## Round 1 — Pain-point discovery (no prototype yet)

Do not mention or hint at ScholarForge's concept in this round — the goal is their unprompted behavior and language.

1. What are you currently working on that needs research sources or citations?
2. What stage are you at — just have an idea, actively searching, or already writing?
3. Walk me through the last time you needed to find a research paper. Where did you start, and what did you actually type into a search box?
4. Have you ever known roughly what you wanted to research but not the right keywords or a specific title? What did you do then?
5. Where do you keep track of papers/sources you've found?
6. How do you currently format citations, and how much time or frustration does that take?
7. Tell me about the last time you sat down to start writing a proposal or paper section from scratch. What was that like?
8. Have you used AI tools (e.g. ChatGPT) for research or writing help? What for, and what made you hesitate, if anything — cost, privacy, accuracy?

*(If this is a Round-1-only interview, skip to Closing below.)*

---

## Round 2 — Prototype walkthrough + reaction

Narrate each step out loud as you click; ask the paired question(s) right after, while the screen is still showing.

| Screen / action | What to say / do | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Login screen** | "This is a local sign-in — your own email/password, no Google or Facebook login." | (no question yet, just note reaction) |
| **My Projects** | Open/create a project. "Each project keeps its own saved papers and outlines completely separate from any other project." | "Does keeping different assignments separate like this match how you'd want to work, or would you rather have one big pile?" |
| **Search screen** | Type **the participant's own real research idea** (from your pre-session ask) into the idea box, run the search. | "Do these results feel relevant to what you meant — even the ones that don't share your exact words?" |
| **Save + Library** | Save 1–2 results, switch the citation style dropdown (APA → IEEE). | "How does this compare to how you currently collect and format sources?" |
| **Outline screen** | Generate the outline. Scroll through a section, point at the bullet ideas + italicized "Starter line," then point at the info banner: *"this is a scaffold, not a finished paper."* | "How do you feel about the app stopping here instead of writing the full paper for you?" |
| **Copy / Download actions** | Show "Copy" on a section and the "Download outline as PDF/Markdown" buttons. | "Would you actually take this and keep writing from it in your own document, or does that handoff feel like a dead end?" |

---

## Adoption & willingness (both rounds)

1. Would you actually use this for your current work? What would stop you?
2. What would make this a "must-have" instead of a "nice-to-have" for you?
3. Who else do you know who'd want this, and how would you describe it to them?

## Closing

1. Anything we didn't ask about that's relevant to how you research or write?
2. Can we follow up once there's a working (non-prototype) version?

---

## Immediately after the session (moderator notes, not read aloud)
- Write down, in the participant's own words, anything that confirms or contradicts H1–H4 (see interview-plan.md) — especially any moment they reacted negatively to the "scaffold, not ghostwriter" boundary, since that's the highest-risk direction check.
- Note their discipline (STEM vs. humanities) next to their citation-style comment, to check if IEEE-as-default holds broadly or only for some fields.
