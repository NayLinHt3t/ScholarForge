Trust in Virtual Teams: How Remote Work Changes Team Dynamics
Leonardi, P., Neeley, T.  ·  2022  ·  Journal of Applied Psychology
This paper investigates how trust forms and erodes in fully remote teams over a 12-month longitudinal study across 24 organisations. We identify three critical phases of trust development unique to remote contexts and propose a dynamic trust model that accounts for asynchronous communication patterns and reduced social cues in distributed workplaces.
remote work team trust virtual teams longitudinal
Psychological Safety and Remote Collaboration: A Meta-Analysis
Newman, A., Donohue, R., Eva, N.  ·  2021  ·  Journal of Organizational Behavior
Meta-analysis of 67 studies (N = 18,402) examining the relationship between psychological safety and team performance in remote and hybrid settings. Results indicate that psychological safety mediates the negative effects of geographic dispersion on team trust and collaborative innovation outcomes.
psychological safety meta-analysis remote collaboration
Asynchronous Communication and the Erosion of Interpersonal Trust in Distributed Teams
Golden, T. D., Veiga, J. F.  ·  2023  ·  MIS Quarterly
A grounded theory study of 38 distributed software teams reveals how the shift from synchronous to asynchronous communication correlates with declining interpersonal trust scores over a 6-month period, and proposes five evidence-based mitigation strategies for remote team managers.
async communication distributed teams trust erosion grounded theory

Title & Abstract

Thesis: Remote Work Trust

  • Define the gap: how trust develops differently in remote vs. co-located teams
  • Scope: longitudinal study of distributed knowledge workers (6 months)
  • Key constructs: interpersonal trust, asynchronous communication, psychological safety
  • Index Terms: remote work, team trust, virtual collaboration, distributed teams
Draft — revise before use

This paper investigates how trust forms in distributed teams, drawing on Leonardi and Neeley [1] and the meta-analytic evidence from Newman et al. [2]. We argue that co-located trust frameworks inadequately capture the async communication dynamics identified by Golden and Veiga [3].

I. Introduction
  • Motivate the study: growth of remote work post-2020 and unanswered trust questions [1]
  • Highlight the gap: cross-sectional studies miss longitudinal trust dynamics [2]
  • State research question: how does async-first communication moderate trust over time? [3]
  • Preview contributions: dynamic trust model, three longitudinal phases, five mitigation strategies
Draft — revise before use

The rapid normalisation of remote work has outpaced the theoretical frameworks used to study it. Leonardi and Neeley [1] identify three trust phases unique to virtual teams, yet the mechanisms remain underspecified—particularly given the psychological safety literature reviewed by Newman et al. [2].

II. Related Work
  • Seminal trust models (Mayer et al. 1995) and their applicability to virtual contexts
  • Virtual team literature: media richness, social presence, swift trust [1]
  • Meta-analytic evidence on psychological safety as a trust mediator [2]
  • Gap: limited longitudinal studies on async communication and trust erosion [3] [4]
Draft — revise before use

Prior work has largely adopted cross-sectional designs. Leonardi and Neeley [1] address this with a longitudinal approach, yet their sample predates async-first tools examined by Golden and Veiga [3] and Kossek et al. [4].

III. Proposed Methodology
  • Mixed-methods longitudinal design: survey waves at T1, T2, T3 (0, 3, 6 months)
  • Sample: 20 fully remote teams (N ≈ 160) across tech and knowledge-work sectors
  • Measures: adapted Mayer trust scale; psychological safety scale from Newman et al. [2]
  • Semi-structured interviews at T2 to surface mechanisms behind quantitative anomalies [1]
  • Analysis: latent growth-curve modelling + thematic coding
Draft — revise before use

We adopt a mixed-methods longitudinal design informed by Leonardi and Neeley [1]. Trust trajectories will be modelled with latent growth-curve analysis, with qualitative data collected at T2 following the triangulation strategy of Newman et al. [2].

IV. Expected Contribution
  • Theoretical: extend dynamic trust models to async-first remote environments [1] [3]
  • Empirical: first longitudinal dataset linking async frequency to trust trajectory
  • Practical: five evidence-based mitigation strategies for remote managers [3]
  • Policy: inform organisational guidelines on hybrid/remote work structure [4]
Draft — revise before use

This study contributes a dynamic longitudinal trust model for async-first teams, extending Leonardi and Neeley [1] and operationalising the psychological safety construct of Newman et al. [2] within a trust context.
